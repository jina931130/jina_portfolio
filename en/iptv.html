Chapter 01

Background and questions

01 · Background

IPTV has grown into a personalised
media hub, yet it is slipping
out of daily life

IPTV has grown into a personalised media hub that brings live channels, VOD, OTT links and AI recommendations together in one place. Yet subscriber growth is at its lowest in years, and fewer people than last year watch TV five or more days a week.

02 · Perspective

We treated leaving the TV off
as a choice users make

Earlier studies focused on external factors such as what drives subscription or how the market competes. This project treats the moment the TV goes off not as failure but as strategic non-use, and looks at avoidance and return as one repeating cycle.

The usual view

Technology failure · rejection · indifference

The cause is sought in poor features or a lack of interest

This project's view

An active choice · strategic avoidance

Emotion, context, relationships and clashing values all play a part

03 · Question

When do people switch IPTV off,
and what brings them back?

To see avoidance and return as one flow rather than two separate things, we started from two questions.

RQ1

In what situations and contexts do IPTV users
avoid the technology and come back to it?

RQ2

How can those patterns of avoidance and return
be grouped into types?

Process

05 · Clustering

We grouped users into four types and chose the two with room to come back

We ran K-means clustering on how often people avoided IPTV and why, what brought them back, how they felt when leaving and returning, and how satisfied they were afterwards. Of the four types, the 'conditional' and 'mixed feelings' groups still saw meaning in IPTV but had stopped using it, so we chose them for the qualitative study.

06 · Findings

What decided the intention to return
was conditions, not feelings

Analysing the 200 responses, the biggest influence on the intention to return to IPTV was whether a condition for returning existed. Those conditions are practical things users name themselves, such as a wider range of content, a simpler interface or live programming. How people felt about TV had no clear link to the intention to return.

Three things the quantitative analysis showed

The main reason people do not use IPTV is that OTT is easier

We asked the 43 non-users why they had stopped. The most common answer was that OTT is easier, followed by a lack of interesting content and the cost.

11 · Zero Burden IPTV

Five design strategies that take the weight
out of switching the TV on

For each factor we pinpointed the moment avoidance begins and proposed a design strategy that removes the burden at that moment. We looked at TV as more than a device for consuming content: as a medium that helps people recover emotionally after a tiring day.

Zero Burden IPTV

Removing the burden of time, emotion, choice, access and relationships

Factor

Avoidance trigger

Design strategy

Space

The TV is far away and even finding the remote feels like too much, so people switch to another device.

The TV turns on automatically when they come home + simple voice and touch controls

A TV that is simply there, minimising avoidance and easing the way back

Time

When a busy schedule or tiredness breaks the routine, TV quietly disappears from the day.

Content recommended from their daily pattern + a reminder to pick the routine back up

Preventing avoidance when the routine breaks, and prompting a return

Emotion

When they are tired or worn out, even turning the TV on feels like emotional work.

Low-stimulus background content plays automatically + a low-effort viewing mode

Blocking emotion-driven avoidance and easing the way back through a sense of calm

Relationships

Channel disputes at home, the pressure of being asked to watch together, and the awkwardness of watching alone all lead to avoidance.

Individual profiles + balanced recommendations across the family

Less pressure in watching together, and conversation as a way back

Technology · content

Content is hard to find and the remote is awkward, so getting started falls apart.

Saved content plays automatically + an interface that minimises searching, with voice and mobile links

Removing the barrier to entry and giving a trigger to watch again

12 · Design Principles

To get people watching again,
start by removing the burden

To take real weight off people emotionally and mentally, we drew four principles for designing the service.

Four principles for a Zero Burden IPTV

01

Minimise mental effort

A UI and UX structure that cuts down choosing, searching and deciding

02

Account for emotional state

Low-effort content that stays reachable even when people are emotionally worn out

03

Adapt to the situation

A service flow that adjusts itself to the time, the place and whether anyone else is there

04

Design for coming back

A service designed to slip back into the rhythm of daily life
