SELECTED PROJECTS

Feb 2026 – Jun 2026

Master's thesis · HMI prompts in high-cognitive-load driving (solo research)

Master's thesis, Graduate School of Communication and Arts, Yonsei University · Aug 2026

Solo research testing how HMI prompts affect drivers' behaviour correction under high cognitive load. Designed a 3×3 repeated-measures experiment with modality and intervention timing as factors, and built the cluster UI, driving scenarios and experiment control programme myself. All 20 participants braked faster when the prompt came later, while 16 of them also reported higher cognitive load; proposed design criteria for prompts by situation.

Sep 2025 – Dec 2025

Online review attribute visualisation (team of 4)

Proceedings of HCI Korea 2026, pp. 1764–1770 · First author · Jan 2026

Team study testing how visualising trust cues in online reviews affects consumer trust and purchase intention. My proposal was chosen as the team topic, so I led the team and took charge of the experiment design and analysis. Thirteen participants went through all six conditions (three visualisation types × two product types) in a repeated-measures study, and the social-cue type scored highest on every measure. Turned the results into four guidelines for review screens.

Mar 2025 – Jun 2025

TA:GO · ride-sharing taxi service for older adults (team of 4)

A ride-sharing call service designed so that older adults with limited mobility can call a taxi on their own, without help from family. In a team of four I handled the app UI, interaction and PBV design, and took part in the interviews and usability tests. We found the barrier was not text size but the lack of a chance to practise, so I designed a practice mode to run before a real call. Finalist, 3rd Kia PBV Idea Competition.

Sep 2024 – Dec 2024

Technology avoidance and return in IPTV use (team of 4)

Journal of Digital Contents Society, Vol. 26, No. 12 (KCI-listed) · Second author · Dec 2025

Team study on why people leave IPTV and come back, even after personalised services became the norm. I proposed the topic and led the quantitative survey design and analysis. Clustering a national survey of 200 users gave four user types, and return intention turned out to be driven by concrete conditions users could name rather than by emotion. Combined with diary and interview data to build an avoidance–return matrix and a low-burden design strategy.

Nov 2023 – Dec 2023
